# Authenticity and accuracy review

Mangrok treats culinary authenticity as contextual, plural, and source-dependent. A reference recipe may be representative, but it must never be presented as the only legitimate version of a dish.

## Review rules

- Record the dish's region, language, and culinary tradition where known.
- Distinguish a raw ingredient, cultivar, processed form, prepared component, and finished dish.
- Preserve regional names and transliterations rather than replacing them with one English label.
- Cite the source used to verify characteristic ingredients, techniques, and cultural context.
- Explain Mangrok's editorial standardization, including serving size, measurements, equipment, and substitutions.
- Mark disputed origin claims, regional variants, and unavailable evidence.
- Do not infer allergen safety, doneness, preservation safety, or medical suitability from cultural context alone.
- Do not use generated images as evidence that a recipe was physically tested.
- Review community additions before promoting them to the published reference table.

## Accuracy states

- `reviewed-reference`: independently written and source-checked.
- `community-proposal`: submitted but not verified.
- `personal`: private user content.
- `tested-by-user`: a user recorded a real cooking outcome.
- `expert-reviewed`: reviewed by a qualified culinary or food-safety specialist.

The UI must display the state rather than blending all records into one confidence level.
